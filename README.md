**Use this when** you need one page that gets one thing done, and you want to
know what belongs on it before anyone writes a word.

## The rule

> One page, one action. A second action does not add a path, it splits the
> first one.

The page answers four questions in order, and every block on it has to be
answering one of them. A block that answers none gets cut, including the one
you like.

| order | the question they are actually asking |
|---|---|
| 1 | What is this |
| 2 | Is it for me |
| 3 | Why should I believe you |
| 4 | What happens if I do the thing |

## The headline is determined by where they came from

This is the part that gets skipped, and it decides the headline more than
anything about your business.

Someone who clicked an ad about hard water spots lands expecting hard water
spots. Someone who typed your name into Google already trusts you and needs the
next step, not a pitch.

So before any copy: where does the traffic come from, and what does it already
know. If that is unknown, it is the first `GUESSED` line and it is load bearing.

## How it works

**1. I name the single action** and say out loud what I am cutting to protect
it.

**2. I check how your buyers actually make contact.** Phone, text, or form, by
looking at how your last five customers first reached you. A form on a page for
people who only ever call collects nothing.

**3. I write the objections in your buyer's words, not mine.** Top three, and
anything I invented is labeled, because a made up objection moves the page away
from the real one.

**4. I use only proof that exists.** If the proof slot is empty the page says
less and asks anyway. It does not borrow credibility.

**5. I label every block,** so you read an outline before anyone writes
polished copy over a guess.

## Limits

I cannot write proof you do not have. No testimonials, no logo bar, no "trusted
by hundreds," no star rating, no client count. If you did not give it to me,
the proof block shrinks and the page still asks for the call.

I cannot tell you if the page will convert. Any conversion number I offered
you would be invented.

I cannot see your traffic. Where visitors come from is the most load bearing
input here, and the one I am least able to check.

**The trap:** a naive version writes a clever headline with no connection to
what the visitor just clicked, stacks three fictional testimonials and a vague
"hundreds of happy customers" under it, and adds a second call to action
because the page felt empty. It looks like a real landing page. It is
unfaithful to the traffic, it lies in the proof block, and the split action
quietly costs you the calls the page was built to get.

## How this one knows what it knows

Every answer it gives itself carries a label: `CHECKED` (it verified it,
and says how), `FROM YOU` (you said it, and it quotes where), or
`GUESSED` (it could not verify). Then it counts the guesses, so you
correct what is wrong instead of trusting confident output built on
silent assumptions.

> Never ask for something you can check.
> Never assume something you cannot check.
